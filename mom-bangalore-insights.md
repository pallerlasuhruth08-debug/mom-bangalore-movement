# What the data actually says

Eight insights from the Ishangam `miracle.of.mind` extract, 31 July 2026 — ranked by how much they should change what you do, not by how interesting they are.

---

## 1. You are not recruiting from a growing community. You are reactivating a cold list.

This is the most important thing in the data and nobody has said it.

| Month | Bengaluru signups |
|---|---|
| **Feb 2025** | **152,309** |
| **Mar 2025** | **61,339** |
| Apr 2025 – Dec 2025 | 3,140 – 9,488 / month |
| Jan 2026 – Jul 2026 | 2,632 – 5,869 / month |

**73% of the entire Bangalore base — 213,648 of 293,569 people — registered in the first two months after launch, seventeen months ago.** Since then the city has added about 5,000 a month, and 2026 is running slower than 2025 at 3,839.

Three consequences:

**The 3% assumption is far more fragile than it looks.** Three percent of an engaged, growing community is a reasonable planning number. Three percent of a seventeen-month-old registration list that has never been contacted is a guess. These are not the same population and the plan currently treats them as if they were.

**Organic growth will not help you.** At the current run rate, August through November adds roughly 15,000 more users — 5% on top of the base. Nothing in the December arithmetic can lean on new signups.

**The campaign's real job is reactivation, not acquisition.** That changes the message. "Come back" is a different ask from "join", and it needs different copy, a different first touch, and a lower expected response rate. Plan for it now rather than discovering it in October.

One bright spot worth chasing: **October 2025 shows 9,488 signups against neighbouring months of 4,224 and 3,537.** Something worked that month — a campaign, an event, a school push. It is the only evidence in eighteen months of a repeatable acquisition lever, and whoever ran it should be found this week.

---

## 2. Miracle of Mind has not penetrated Isha's own base

Bangalore has **78,620 meditators** in Ishangam. Only **30,791** of them appear as Miracle of Mind users.

Matching is imperfect — unmatched MOM records cannot carry the meditator flag — so the honest range is that **somewhere between 26,000 and 48,000 Bangalore meditators are not on the app.**

Nobody in the 30 July meeting mentioned this pool, and it is the cheapest one in the entire plan:

- They already meditate. No persuasion needed about the practice.
- They are already contactable through their centre — no in-app access required, no dependency on the 1 November date.
- Getting them onto the app **directly produces December minutes**, independent of whether they ever volunteer.
- It is a defensible ask for centre coordinators who may resist a general volunteering drive: *get your own people onto the app* is squarely their job.

If a third of that pool installs and meditates through December, it is worth roughly a million minutes on its own — comparable to the entire uplift-on-existing-base lever in the phase plan, for far less effort.

**Recommendation: make this a named workstream with a centre-wise target. It is currently in nobody's plan.**

---

## 3. Two "centres" are not centres, and they are 21% of the base

| Centre | MOM users | Active meditators | Users per meditator | Already meditators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bangalore Rural** | 43,639 | 793 | **55.0** | **1.8%** |
| **Budigere Cross** | 17,610 | 1,366 | **12.9** | **1.0%** |
| *Every real centre* | *405–8,362* | *1,131–4,591* | *0.3–6.5* | *29–39%* |

The city norm is about two app users per active meditator and roughly a third already being meditators. These two sit at 10–30× the ratio with almost no meditator overlap, and Budigere Cross has 100% of its users first logging in during 2026.

**Together they hold 61,249 users — 21% of the Bangalore base.** They are bulk-loaded lists, not communities. Any per-centre volunteer target that includes them at face value will be wrong by a factor of ten, and any leaderboard will be won by a centre that did nothing.

They are already excluded from the seeds. What is still needed is an answer to *where did these records come from*, because until that is known, the 21% they represent cannot be counted as either real or fake.

---

## 4. Strip those two out and the centre model is unusually consistent

Every genuine Bangalore centre sits between **1.6 and 3.1** app users per active meditator, with **29–39%** meditator overlap. Jayanagar at 6.5 is the only mild outlier.

That tightness is a gift. It means a per-centre model actually generalises — you can set a target for one centre from another centre's performance and expect it to hold. Most volunteer planning has to assume this and hope; here it is measurable.

It also means the one genuine anomaly is visible: **Girinagar, at 0.3 app users per active meditator.** 1,239 active meditators, only 405 MOM users — seven times below the city norm, in the wrong direction. Either that centre never pushed the app or something structural is blocking it. It is the single cheapest fix available: a centre with a strong meditator base and almost no app presence, needing one conversation rather than a campaign.

## 5. Referral behaviour is broad, not superstar-driven — which is good news

4,322 Bangalore referrers produced 12,367 referrals.

| Cohort | Share of all referrals |
|---|---|
| Top 10 referrers | 9.5% |
| Top 100 | 22.5% |
| Top 500 | 46.9% |

The median referrer referred exactly **one** person. The single largest referred 496.

There is a power law, but a mild one — no tiny elite carrying everything. That matters for the pin-charge model: **the 1,088 people who have referred three or more are a genuinely broad recruiting base**, not a handful of super-connectors who will be over-asked and burn out. A model that depends on thousands of ordinary people each bringing a few others is exactly what this distribution supports.

It also argues for the plan of record's two-currency design. Rewarding *teams spawned* separately from *minutes* is aimed precisely at converting median referrers — people who have already brought one person — into people who bring five. That is where the volume is, not at the top of the list.

## 6. Consent is not the constraint. Contact data is.

**417 people out of 293,569 are on do-not-disturb.** One in seven hundred. Effectively nobody has opted out, and 99.6% carry an explicit opt-in.

But **42% have no phone number on record.** So the wall between you and the base is not permission or goodwill — it is missing contact data. That reframes the 1 November in-app access date: it is not a convenience or a channel preference, it is the only route to 123,000 people you are otherwise structurally unable to reach.

It also suggests a cheap parallel play: every touchpoint between now and December — consecration, IE closings, school sessions, pincode meetups — should capture a phone number as a first-class action, not an afterthought. Every number captured permanently converts an unreachable user into a reachable one.

---

## 7. The volunteer target survives contact with the data, but only just

Two independent methods now agree on the shape:

- Pincode tiers, built bottom-up from 71,750 real records: **8,806**
- The residual needed to reach 10,000: **1,194** — which lands almost exactly on the 1,200 the grassroots plan had already assigned to consecration, IE closings and walk-ins

That agreement is the first external check the 10,000 has ever had, and it passes. But the margin is thin and it depends entirely on reaching the full base. Without in-app notification the realistic ceiling is **7,859**, and no amount of ground effort closes a 2,141-person gap in eighteen weeks.

Read together with insight 1 — that the base is cold, not warm — the sensible posture is: **hold 10,000 as the public number, plan the ground structure for 7,859, and treat everything above that as contingent on in-app access landing on time.**

---

## 8. The structure is over-built at the bottom and under-built at the top

Twenty pincodes carry half the target. Eighty-seven carry a fifth between them.

A flat hundred-captain grid spends the same recruiting effort on a pincode worth 12 volunteers as on one worth 369. Cutting to roughly 63 — dedicated captains with cell-leader depth in Tier 1, one each in Tier 2, Tier 3 clustered into about fifteen multi-pincode captaincies — redeploys that capacity where the volunteers actually are.

And the top of the structure can be staffed from people already doing the job: **1,088 Bangalore users have referred three or more people with no campaign, no incentive and nobody asking. Every one of the twenty Tier 1 pincodes has at least five.** Twenty-six pincodes have none and must be sourced from their centre.

---

## What I would do first, given all of the above

1. **Find out what happened in October 2025.** It is the only repeatable acquisition lever visible in eighteen months of data.
2. **Open the meditator-onboarding workstream.** 26,000–48,000 Bangalore meditators are not on the app. Cheapest minutes and cheapest volunteers in the plan, and it has no dependency on 1 November.
3. **Get an answer on Bangalore Rural and Budigere Cross.** 21% of the base is currently uncountable.
4. **Reframe the campaign internally as reactivation.** The base is seventeen months cold. Copy, first touch and expected response rate all follow from that, and getting it wrong is invisible until October.
5. **Escalate in-app access this week.** It is the difference between 7,859 and 10,000.
