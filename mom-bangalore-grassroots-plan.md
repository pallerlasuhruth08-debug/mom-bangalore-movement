# Grassroots Volunteer Mobilisation — Bangalore
### The volunteer track: how 10,000 actually gets built

**Drafted:** 31 July 2026 · **Owners:** Suhruth + Santosh · **Target:** ~10,000 active volunteers by 15 November 2026

---

## 1. The argument this plan is built on

**The 18 Bangalore centres cannot produce 10,000 volunteers. Not because the team is weak — because the pool isn't there.**

Bangalore has roughly 35,000 meditators active in the last six months across all Isha programs. Historical volunteering conversion from a meditator base runs 3–8%:

| Conversion assumption | Volunteers from centre ecosystem |
|---|---|
| 3% (Yoga Veera-like) | ~1,050 |
| 5% (realistic good run) | ~1,750 |
| 8% (best case ever achieved) | ~2,800 |

So a centre-only strategy tops out around **2,000–2,800**. To hit 10,000 through centres alone, roughly **30% of every active meditator in Bangalore** would have to become a campaign volunteer. That has never happened anywhere, in any Isha campaign, ever.

**Conclusion: grassroots mobilisation is not a sub-tactic of the centre plan. It is a parallel structure, organised by geography rather than by affiliation.** The centres become the *spine* — they train, hold and support — but roughly three-quarters of the volunteers must come from outside the centre-affiliated base.

### Where the other 7,500 are hiding

**In the app.** Bangalore already has ~300–350K monthly Miracle of Mind users. You need 10,000 volunteers. That is **3% of people who already meditate on your product every month** — and you have a direct notification channel to every one of them. This never came up in the meeting; the entire discussion assumed volunteers must come from the Ishanga/centre system.

| Pool | Size | Conversion | Volunteers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isha centre ecosystem (Ishangas, active meditators, centre volunteers) | ~35,000 | 5% | **1,750** |
| **Miracle of Mind Bangalore app users** | **~320,000** | **2.5%** | **8,000** |
| Consecration, IE class closings, event walk-ins | ~15,000 touched | 8% | **1,200** |
| | | | **~10,950** |

The app pool carries the plan. The centre pool provides the leadership layer. Do not mix up which does which.

## 2. Redefine what a volunteer is

This is the second reason 10,000 felt impossible in the room. Everyone was picturing a Yoga Veera — trained, committed, taking leave, running formal sessions. On that definition, 10,000 is fantasy: Yoga Veera went 1,000 registered → 350 trained → **60 who actually ran a session**.

For this campaign, redefine the base unit:

> **A volunteer is someone who hosts one seven-minute sit for ten people.**

That is a twenty-minute commitment. No leave. No training course. No travel. Your neighbour, your team at work, your parents and their friends, your morning walk group. On *that* definition, 3% of your app base is entirely reasonable — and it directly addresses the consecration-fatigue problem raised in the meeting, because nobody is being asked for days of their life.

**Four tiers, each with a different ask:**

| Tier | Count | The ask | Time cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Host** | ~10,000 | Run 2 sits of ~10 people in December | ~1 hour total |
| **Cell leader** | ~1,000 | Hold a patch (apartment, street, office floor); recruit and hold ~10 hosts | ~2 hrs/week, Oct–Dec |
| **Pincode captain** | ~100 | Hold one Bangalore pincode; hold ~10 cell leaders | ~5 hrs/week, Sep–Dec |
| **Centre anchor** | ~54 | 3 per centre; hold ~2 pincodes each; train and support | Existing seva load |

**18 centres → 100 pincode captains → 1,000 cell leaders → 10,000 hosts.** Clean 10× at each layer. Nobody at any layer is being asked to do something unprecedented, and the bottom layer — where all the volume is — is being asked for twenty minutes.

Output: 10,000 hosts × 2 sits × 10 people × 7 min = **1.4 million minutes**, plus the recruitment effect on app usage.

## 3. Geography, not affiliation

Use **pincode** as the organising unit, not centre catchment. Bangalore has roughly 100 active urban pincodes. Every volunteer gets a named patch they can point at.

You already have the infrastructure for this: the pincode→centre map built into the E-City nurturing app. Extend it city-wide and it becomes the campaign's operating backbone — every host, cell and captain resolves to a pincode, every pincode rolls up to a centre, every centre rolls up to the city total.

**Why geography beats affiliation for grassroots work:**
- A person can name their pincode; they often can't name their nearest Isha centre
- It creates natural rivalry — pincode leaderboards, "which part of Bangalore is calmest"
- It exposes white space. A centre map hides the fact that nobody is working Whitefield; a pincode map makes it a visible hole
- It lets non-Isha people lead. A cell leader in HSR who has never been to a centre can still hold a patch

### The cell: the actual grassroots unit

A **cell** is 5–10 hosts sharing a physical context. Six types, in descending order of yield:

1. **Apartment / gated community cell** — the highest-yield unit in Bangalore. One resident meditator → RWA permission → clubhouse sit → 30–50 people. Bangalore's density in apartments is the campaign's single biggest structural advantage
2. **Workplace cell** — one champion per floor or team in the tech parks; lunch-hour sits
3. **Campus cell** — students, tied to college festivals
4. **Street / layout cell** — for non-apartment neighbourhoods; park sits, resident associations
5. **School-parent cell** — the school worksheet already asks children to collect family signatures. That is a parent-recruitment engine sitting unused. Convert signing parents into hosts
6. **Affinity cell** — running and cycling clubs (Bangalore's running community is large and organised, and they already practise in groups), yoga groups, chess clubs, temple committees, senior citizen groups

Each type gets a one-page SOP and a WhatsApp-forwardable kit. If a cell leader can't run it from the kit alone without a phone call, the kit isn't finished.

## 4. The 90-day build

### Stage 1 — Seed the leadership layer · 1 Aug – 30 Sep
**Goal: 54 anchors, 100 pincode captains.** These come almost entirely from the centre ecosystem — this is what centres are *for*.

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Consecration harvest — start this week** | ~2,000 volunteers arriving on e-passes for 7–11 days. Intent capture via QR at the end of each day's seva: name, pincode, what they can offer. **Target 1,000 intents by 17 Aug.** This window closes in two weeks and it is the single richest recruiting ground before December |
| **Every IE class closing** | Pranathi's experiment: one appeal at one closing yielded 65–70 inquiries. Make it standard at *every* closing across Bangalore from August. At ~2 closings/week × 60 inquiries, that's ~500 leads by end September |
| **Centre kickoff, ~20 Aug** | All 18 centre coordinators. Each picks its own pincode targets — the mechanic that made the Bhikshu centre-wise meet work. Each nominates 3 anchors on the spot |
| **Pincode assignment** | Map all ~100 pincodes to centres. Publish the map. Every unassigned pincode is a visible gap |
| **Captain onboarding** | Each captain claims their pincode on the platform *the day they sign up*. If they leave without a page of their own, you have a name on a list, not a captain |

**Exit gate 30 Sep: 100 pincodes each with a named captain. Zero unassigned pincodes.**

### Stage 2 — Build the cell layer · 1 Oct – 31 Oct
**Goal: 1,000 cell leaders.** Each captain recruits 10. Sourced from centre lists, consecration intents, and the first in-app push.

- Captains work their own pincode: which apartments, which offices, which colleges, which parks
- **Weekly captain call, same day and time every week.** The cadence is the management system — a captain who misses two consecutive calls is functionally gone and should be replaced, not chased indefinitely
- **Pilot three cell types in three pincodes in early October** before scaling. Document what actually happens, not what should happen

**Exit gate 31 Oct: 1,000 cell leaders, each holding a named patch.**

### Stage 3 — The host wave · 1 Nov – 30 Nov
**Goal: 10,000 hosts.** This is where the app carries the load.

- **In-app campaign to all Bangalore users.** "Bangalore is going for 10 million minutes in December. Will you host one seven-minute sit?" One tap to say yes; it asks for a pincode and routes them to the local cell leader. This is the mechanism that makes 10,000 possible — everything before it is building the structure to catch them
- Cell leaders convert their own networks in parallel
- **Every new host gets contacted by a human within 48 hours** and does their first practice sit within 7 days (see §5)

**Exit gate 30 Nov: 10,000 hosts, each with a December date in the calendar and a kit in hand.**

## 5. The drop-off problem — where grassroots campaigns actually die

Yoga Veera: 1,000 registered → 350 trained → **60 conducted**. The catastrophic loss is not at signup and not at training. It is between *trained* and *did the thing once*.

**Time-to-first-action is the single best predictor of whether a volunteer stays.** Three interventions, in order of importance:

1. **First sit within 7 days of signing up**, with the cell leader present. Not in December — in the week they join. A host who has run one sit in October will run two in December. A host who has only been trained will run zero.
2. **A human call within 48 hours of signup.** Not a WhatsApp broadcast. Someone whose name they know. This is what Smrithi's school finding says in different words: the school with daily visits massively outperformed the school with a single visit. Presence, not information.
3. **Visible attribution from day one.** Every host sees their own number, their cell's number, their pincode's rank. Bhikshu's enrolment measurably dropped when the micro-pages weren't ready. Do not repeat that.

**Assume 40% attrition between signup and December and recruit against that.** To have 10,000 active hosts on 1 December, sign up ~14,000. Any plan that assumes everyone who says yes shows up will miss by half.

## 6. Feet on street — what grassroots actually looks like

Shashank's insistence that a movement means people physically on the street, not efficient calling, should shape the tactics. High-yield, low-cost activities that also *recruit* while they run:

| Activity | Where | Recruits |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning park sits** | Cubbon, Lalbagh, and every neighbourhood park — Bangalore's morning-walk culture is a ready-made audience | Walkers → hosts |
| **Apartment clubhouse sits** | Via RWAs; one resident opens the door | Residents → cell leaders |
| **Tech park lunch sits** | Lobby or lawn, 12:30–2pm | Employees → workplace cells |
| **Weekly "Calm Sunday"** | Every pincode, same time each Sunday from October — builds rhythm before December | Everyone |
| **Market and high-street booths** | With brand partner activation (boAt-style listening booths) where available | Public → hosts |
| **College fest presence** | October–November festival season | Students → campus cells |
| **SSB and temple footfall** | 10–15K on a normal weekend, 25–30K on long weekends | Visitors → hosts, by pincode |

Every one of these is both a *minutes* activity and a *recruitment* activity. Every sit should end with the same line: "Will you host one of these where you live?"

## 7. Gamification — two layers, not one

The meeting correctly identified that recruitment and delivery need separate game mechanics.

**Layer 1 — recruitment (Aug–Nov).** The currency is people.
- Every tier sees their downline count live
- Pincode leaderboard: captains ranked by cells built
- Milestone recognition at 5 / 10 / 25 hosts recruited
- Centre-level board so the 18 centres compete visibly

**Layer 2 — delivery (December).** The currency is minutes.
- Pincode leaderboard on minutes, with a **per-capita** view alongside the absolute one, so small pincodes can win too
- **Weekly resets** so someone joining on 15 December can still top a board
- Team/cell identity — self-named teams of 3–11, which is what makes corporate step-challenges outperform individual ones
- Publish **unique participants** alongside minutes. Minutes alone will be dominated by a few hundred devoted meditators; breadth is what makes it a movement

**Inspiration over incentive.** The point made in the meeting stands — this has to be volunteering. The game should make contribution *visible*, not purchasable. Recognition, territory and rank; not rewards.

## 8. What this needs from other tracks

The grassroots plan is blocked on three things it does not control. Flag these on Saturday:

1. **The platform, live by 30 September.** Target-claim pages, group-sit logging, downline visibility. If it slips, Stage 2 recruits cell leaders with nothing to claim and they go cold before December. *This is exactly how Bhikshu lost enrolments.* Minimum viable version if time is short: target-claim page + group-sit logging. Leaderboards can follow in November.
2. **In-app access to the Bangalore user base by 1 November.** This is not a nice-to-have — 8,000 of the 10,000 come through it. Needs product and MOM core to commit now.
3. **The cause name by 10 August.** Kits, scripts, RWA pitches and college decks are all blocked on it. A good-enough name in August beats a perfect one in September.
