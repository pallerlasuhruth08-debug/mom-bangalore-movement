# Captain Incentives — Designed to Recruit, Not Just Reward
### MOM Bangalore · ~100 pincode captains, ~1,000 cell leaders

---

## The design rule

Someone in the 30 July meeting said it correctly and then the discussion moved on: *"if you do only incentive, the problem is this has to be volunteering… it has to be inspiration."* That instinct is right but it's usually applied as "so give them nothing." That's not the answer either — 100 captains working five hours a week for four months need something real.

The resolution is a design test, not a moral position:

> **Can a bystander see it, and can they get it?**
>
> **Visible + attainable = recruits.** A badge on the ground, a pincode rank on a public board, a pass the captain hands to their neighbour.
> **Invisible or unattainable = doesn't recruit.** A private gift, a voucher, a prize only #1 can win.

You asked for incentives that inspire *others* to join. That rules out anything the captain consumes privately. **The best captain incentive is something they can give away.** A reward that flows *through* the captain to their cell makes them a person of consequence in their locality — and every time they hand it over, it's a recruitment conversation they didn't have to force.

**Second rule: threshold, not rank.** Rank creates drama; thresholds create behaviour. If the Sadhguru interaction goes to the top three captains, ninety-seven disengage by week two. If it goes to everyone who builds ten cells, all hundred push. Use leaderboards for glory and thresholds for rewards — never the reverse.

---

## Tier 1 — Transmissible rewards (the recruiting engine)

These are the ones that answer your actual question. The captain doesn't receive them; they *distribute* them.

**Event passes to give away.** Each captain gets an allocation — say 10–20 — of passes to the Sadhguru Bangalore events, to distribute in their pincode as they see fit. Costs you seats you were filling anyway. Turns a captain into someone people in their apartment block want to know. Every pass is a conversation that ends with "and would you host a sit?"

**Program scholarships to gift.** A small number of free or heavily subsidised Inner Engineering / Isha program seats per captain, per month, to give to someone in their cell. This is the highest-value thing in the list and it costs marginal seats, not money. It also feeds your own funnel — the recipients become the next wave of hosts.

**Waitlist priority to allocate.** The transcript notes advanced program waitlists where "nobody's getting in." A captain who can move someone up a waitlist has real standing. Give each captain a quota of nominations rather than a personal skip.

**Prasad and consecrated items to distribute.** Small, meaningful things to hand out at the end of each sit — Linga Bhairavi prasad, rudraksha, a consecrated thread. Low cost, high emotional weight, and it makes every one of the 20,000 December sits end with a physical touchpoint the participant takes home.

**Nomination rights.** A captain can nominate one person from their pincode for reserved Makar Sankranti seating, or for "Volunteer of the Month" on the campaign's channels. The power to elevate someone else is a stronger motivator than being elevated.

## Tier 2 — Access (highest value, near-zero cost)

**Captains' satsang with Sadhguru.** A dedicated interaction — even 30 minutes — for captains who cross the threshold. This is the single most powerful currency Isha has and it costs a slot in an existing Bangalore visit. *Needs governance approval; flag early, don't assume.*

**Reserved seating at Makar Sankranti** for the captain plus two guests. This closes the loop on the whole campaign — the thing December was built for.

**Volunteer/backstage access at the five Sadhguru events.** Being visibly *inside* the operation, not in the audience.

**Named seat at the consecration** for captains onboarded in August.

Access works because it's aspirational and visible — people see who was in the room. It fails if it's arbitrary. Publish the threshold in advance.

---

## Tier 3 — Status, territory, identity

**A named patch.** "Captain — 560066." Public, on the pincode map, on the leaderboard. Territory is the most underrated motivator in grassroots work: people defend a patch they've been named to in a way they never defend a target.

**Something visible on the ground.** A campaign scarf, band or jacket that identifies a captain at any sit. It makes them findable at a park sit and it makes bystanders want one. This is the cheapest recruiting device in the entire plan.

**Name on the city record.** If the Guinness or city-record route is taken, every captain's name goes on the submission roll. Permanent, shareable, costs nothing.

**Captain of the Week** on campaign channels — a real story from a real pincode. Recognition that doubles as content.

---

## Tier 4 — Growth (what they keep afterwards)

**Community-organising training with a real certificate** — running a team, public speaking, local mobilisation. Valuable outside Isha, which matters more than people admit for the 25–35 cohort you most want as captains.

**A named senior guide.** Each captain paired with an experienced Ishanga who calls them weekly. This doubles as the management cadence from the grassroots plan — the support *is* the reward.

**First right on the next thing.** Captains who deliver get first call on the Makar Sankranti core team and on whatever comes after. Continuity is what stops your 10,000 from dissolving on 1 January.

---

## What not to do

**No cash, vouchers, or merchandise as prizes.** The moment a captain can price their contribution, you've capped it at that price, and you've converted seva into a transaction you now have to keep paying for.

**No top-three-only competitions.** Smrithi's school finding applies directly: certificates for everyone, physical ones for the top three, *specifically so losing doesn't damage anyone* — in a campaign about mental wellbeing, a design that makes 97 people feel like losers is self-contradicting.

**Nothing that can't be given to everyone who clears the bar.** If you can't afford the reward at 100 captains, don't offer it at 10.

**No reward for signing up.** Reward the first *action*, not the commitment. This directly attacks the Yoga Veera death zone — 1,000 registered, 350 trained, 60 who actually ran a session. Make the very first threshold "ran one sit," not "attended training."

## The ladder

Thresholds, published in advance, achievable by all 100.

| Level | Threshold | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Anchored** | Pincode claimed + first sit run + 3 cell leaders | Scarf/badge · name on the public pincode map · captains' WhatsApp group |
| **2 · Rooted** | 10 cell leaders held | 10 event passes to distribute · waitlist nomination quota · named senior guide |
| **3 · Rising** | 100 hosts active in the pincode | Captains' satsang with Sadhguru · 3 program scholarships to gift · Captain of the Week eligibility |
| **4 · Bengaluru Builder** | Pincode hits its December minutes target | Reserved Makar Sankranti seating +2 · name on the city record roll · first right on the next campaign |

Cell leaders get a compressed version of the same ladder — badge, passes to give, and eligibility for the captains' satsang at the top tier. The ladder must be visible to hosts too, so the tier above is always something they can see themselves reaching.

| Event pass allocation (~1,500–2,000 passes) | Events team | 15 Aug |

| Makar Sankranti reserved seating block | Sankranti team | 30 Sep |
| Scarves, badges, prasad kits — production and budget | Campaign ops | 31 Aug, to ship with Stage 1 |

The only real budget line here is physical items — scarves, badges, prasad. Everything else is access and allocation you already control. That's the point of designing incentives this way: the things that recruit hardest are the things that cost least.
