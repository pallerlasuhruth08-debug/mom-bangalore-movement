## 1. Where the discussion started

Three threads were on the table, and the first half of the meeting was about whether they are one thing or three:

1. **Sadhguru's five Bangalore events** — original guidance was five conversations with youth icons over roughly 10–12 days, to activate Bangalore's youth and pull them toward Makar Sankranti. Sadhguru's initial suggestion was four events with Rashmika Mandanna plus one with Smriti Mandhana; the team revised this to five personalities with a Bangalore link but pan-India reach, spanning sport, film, comedy and corporate.
2. **Miracle of Mind as an on-ground movement** — the college-competition idea evolved, and Sadhguru's guidance was that it needs to happen as an on-ground movement, piloted in Bangalore.
3. **Makar Sankranti** as the culminating event.

The meeting's most important move was collapsing these: the five events stop being a separate campaign and become **assets inside one overarching Bangalore campaign**.

## 2. Key debates and where they landed

**Youth-only, or all of Bangalore?**
Resolved as: youth is the *multiplier*, not the audience ceiling. The argument that carried was that culture moves through the young — Youth & Truth brought older people to Isha too, and every Sadhguru initiative gets its groundswell from youth. But a one-month, city-wide number will not come from students alone (the app's own base skews 35+), so the events should span founders, corporates and families as well as colleges. Roughly three of five in colleges, two open to the city.

**Celebrity content vs. student content**
Swami Nakshatra's challenge — that celebrity conversations travel as buzz but *student* interactions are what people actually remember from Youth & Truth (JNU, NALSAR) — was accepted as valid. Direction: keep celebrity conversations as the "offbeat" layer that creates attention, and make direct student interaction the main college format. Someone should pull the actual Youth & Truth performance data to settle this properly.

**What is the cause?**
Mental health was floated (launch on World Mental Health Day, 10 Oct, with a Bangalore mental-health study to justify the focus). It was then substantially picked apart: for under-25s "mental health" is already normalised, feels like a serious-illness frame, and is something they think they are *already* handling by avoiding toxicity. Alternatives floated: stress-free Bengaluru, mental fitness, "coolest, calmest city," compulsiveness to consciousness, Guinness-record framing. **Not resolved.**

**The mechanic that unlocked the room**
Sadhguru announces that Bangalore will meditate for N million minutes in December — a city record, not an app campaign. Celebrities pledge their seven minutes and nominate the next person (ice-bucket mechanic). It does not have to be on the Miracle of Mind app. Rationale: taking the app out of the ask makes it democratic, makes it something Sadhguru and celebrities can rally around without it reading as a product push, and downloads follow anyway. A **Google Health partnership** was proposed to reinforce the neutral framing.

## 4. The two numbers that do not yet add up

**A. The volunteer engine delivers roughly a seventh of the target.**
The math agreed in the room: 10,000 volunteers × 2 events each × ~10 people per event = 200,000 people reached; at 7 minutes each that is **1.4 million minutes** — about 14% of a 10-million target. The other ~86% has to come from digital, the events, corporates, and repeat meditation by the existing base. That bridge was never drawn. Either the per-volunteer expectation rises sharply (multiple sessions, larger groups), or the non-volunteer levers need their own numbers.

**B. Nobody netted out the baseline.**
Bangalore already produces roughly **4 million minutes a month** organically (~300–350K monthly users, ~12 min average session). If that holds, a 10M December is only about **6M incremental** — which makes the target far more reachable than the room assumed. The flip side: if 10M is announced as an achievement, ~40% of it was going to happen regardless. Worth deciding deliberately whether the headline number is gross or incremental before it goes to Sadhguru.

**C. 10,000 volunteers is a 17–160× jump, not a 10× one.**
Historical base rates raised in the meeting: Yoga Veera — 1,000 registered → 350 trained → **60 actually conducted sessions**. Bhikshu campaign — ~1,000 signed up → **600+ genuinely active**. Inner Engineering Wave — ~300–400 on ground on a peak day, ~2,000–2,500 involved across all functions over several months. Shashank's point stands and was not answered: scaling existing tactics harder will not produce 10,000. The two things that historically *did* move the number are (i) a productised micro-target page each volunteer can own — the Bhikshu campaign's enrolment visibly dropped when those pages were not ready — and (ii) centre-level ownership, where each centre picks its own target.

## 5. Product requirement (identified as make-or-break)

A campaign platform where an individual, a **team** (3–11 people, self-named), a centre, a college or a corporate can take a minutes target, track contribution, and see progress against a public total. Explicitly modelled on the Bhikshu micro-page mechanic and on corporate step-challenges, where *team* challenges outperform individual ones. Distribution and product were argued to be inseparable — you cannot ask 10,000 volunteers to "get meditation minutes" without a concrete, trackable thing for them to do.

## 6. Risks flagged

- **Volunteer bandwidth.** Consecration volunteering runs ~11 days and people's leave is exhausted; asking the same pool for a December push is a real constraint. Mitigation discussed: design asks that do not need leave (call ten friends, host a weekend group sit), and use the consecration itself as the seeding moment.
- **Lead time.** Rally for Rivers took roughly 3–4 months from guidance to activation. Working backwards from 1 December, centre briefings need to start **second half of August**, not October.
- **Whale skew.** A minutes-based target will be dominated by a small group of heavy meditators. Accepted as tolerable, but the "school of fish" — breadth of participation — is what makes it a movement, so a breadth metric should sit alongside the minutes number.
- **Nothing in Sadhguru's remarks yet ties the events to the call-to-action.** If he does not make the ask on stage, the celebrity and volunteer messaging has to carry it.
