MOM Bangalore — volunteer mobilisation

Live from Ishangam miracle.of.mind (2,392,695 records nationally), queried 31 Jul 2026, joined to res.partner and the Ishangam meditator baseline (10 Jul 2026). Target: 10,000 active Bangalore volunteers by 1 December 2026.

Where do 10,000 volunteers come from?

The 30 July meeting assumed the centre system. It cannot get there. The app base can — but only the part of it we can actually contact.
The one number that carries the argument
Insight 1 — the one that changes the plan

You are not recruiting from a growing community. You are reactivating a cold list.

73% of the entire Bangalore base — 213,648 of 293,569 people — registered in the first two months after launch, seventeen months ago. Since then the city has added about 5,000 a month, and 2026 is running slower than 2025 at 3,839.

  • The 3% assumption is far more fragile than it looks. Three percent of an engaged, growing community is a reasonable planning number. Three percent of a seventeen-month-old registration list that has never been contacted is a guess. The plan treats them as the same population.
  • Organic growth will not help. At the current run rate, August–November adds roughly 15,000 users — 5% on the base. Nothing in the December arithmetic can lean on new signups.
  • The campaign's real job is reactivation, not acquisition. "Come back" is a different ask from "join" — different copy, different first touch, lower expected response. Plan for it now rather than discovering it in October.
One bright spot worth chasing.October 2025 shows 9,488 signups against neighbouring months of 4,224 and 3,537. Something worked that month. It is the only evidence in eighteen months of a repeatable acquisition lever, and whoever ran it should be found this week.
Insight 2 — the cheapest pool, currently in nobody's plan

Miracle of Mind has not penetrated Isha's own base

Bangalore has 78,620 meditators in Ishangam. Only 30,791 appear as MOM users. Matching is imperfect, so the honest range is that 26,000–48,000 Bangalore meditators are not on the app.

  • They already meditate — no persuasion needed about the practice
  • They are contactable through their centre — no dependency on the 1 November in-app date
  • Getting them on the app directly produces December minutes, whether or not they ever volunteer
  • It is a defensible ask for coordinators who may resist a general volunteering drive — get your own people onto the app is squarely their job

If a third of that pool installs and meditates through December it is worth roughly a million minutes on its own — comparable to the entire uplift-on-existing-base lever in the phase plan, for far less effort. Recommendation: make this a named workstream with a centre-wise target.

The city norm is about two app users per active meditator and roughly a third already being meditators. These two sit at 10–30× the ratio with almost no meditator overlap. Together they hold 61,249 users — 21% of the Bangalore base. Any per-centre target that includes them at face value will be wrong by a factor of ten, and any leaderboard will be won by a centre that did nothing. They are excluded from the seeds; what is still missing is an answer to where these records came from.

Insight 4 — and the anomaly it reveals

Strip those two out and the centre model is unusually consistent

Every genuine Bangalore centre sits between 1.6 and 3.1 app users per active meditator, with 29–39% meditator overlap. Jayanagar at 6.5 is the only mild outlier. That tightness is a gift — a per-centre model actually generalises, so you can set one centre's target from another's performance and expect it to hold.

It also makes the one genuine anomaly visible: Girinagar, at 0.3 app users per active meditator — 1,239 active meditators but only 405 MOM users, seven times below the norm in the wrong direction. Either the centre never pushed the app or something structural is blocking it. The cheapest fix available: one conversation, not a campaign.

Insight 5 — good news for the pin-charge model

Referral behaviour is broad, not superstar-driven

4,322 Bangalore referrers produced 12,367 referrals. Top 10 account for 9.5%, top 100 for 22.5%, top 500 for 46.9%. The median referrer referred exactly one person; the largest referred 496.

A power law, but a mild one — no tiny elite carrying everything. The 1,088 people who have referred three or more are a genuinely broad recruiting base, not a handful of super-connectors who will be over-asked and burn out. A model depending on thousands of ordinary people each bringing a few others is exactly what this distribution supports.

It also argues for the plan of record's two-currency design. Rewarding teams spawned separately from minutes aims precisely at converting median referrers — people who have already brought one person — into people who bring five. That is where the volume is, not at the top of the list.

Insight 6 — reframes the 1 Nov date

Consent is not the constraint. Contact data is.

417 people out of 293,569 are on do-not-disturb — one in seven hundred — and 99.6% carry an explicit opt-in. But 42% have no phone number on record. The wall between you and the base is not permission or goodwill; it is missing contact data. In-app access is therefore not a channel preference, it is the only route to 123,000 people you are otherwise structurally unable to reach.

Cheap parallel play: every touchpoint between now and December — consecration, IE closings, school sessions, pincode meetups — should capture a phone number as a first-class action. Every number captured permanently converts an unreachable user into a reachable one.

Insight 7 — the target passes its first real check

10,000 survives contact with the data, but only just

Two independent methods agree: pincode tiers built bottom-up from 71,750 real records give 8,806, and the residual needed to reach 10,000 is 1,194 — almost exactly the 1,200 the grassroots plan had already assigned to consecration, IE closings and walk-ins. That is the first external check the number has ever had, and it passes.

But the margin is thin and depends on reaching the full base. Without in-app notification the ceiling is 7,859, and no amount of ground effort closes a 2,141-person gap in eighteen weeks. Read with Insight 1 — the base is cold, not warm — the sensible posture is: hold 10,000 as the public number, plan the ground structure for 7,859, and treat everything above that as contingent on in-app access landing on time.

Insight 8 — structure

Over-built at the bottom, under-built at the top

Twenty pincodes carry half the target; 87 carry a fifth between them. A flat hundred-captain grid spends the same effort on a pincode worth 12 volunteers as on one worth 369. Cutting to ~63 — dedicated captains with cell-leader depth in Tier 1, one each in Tier 2, Tier 3 clustered into ~15 multi-pincode captaincies — redeploys that capacity where the volunteers are. And the top can be staffed from people already doing the job: 1,088 have referred 3+ people unprompted, and every Tier 1 pincode has at least five.

What I would do first

  1. Find out what happened in October 2025. The only repeatable acquisition lever visible in eighteen months of data.
  2. Open the meditator-onboarding workstream. 26,000–48,000 Bangalore meditators are not on the app. Cheapest minutes and cheapest volunteers available, with no dependency on 1 November.
  3. Get an answer on Bangalore Rural and Budigere Cross. 21% of the base is currently uncountable.
  4. Reframe the campaign internally as reactivation. The base is seventeen months cold. Getting this wrong is invisible until October.
  5. Escalate in-app access this week. It is the difference between 7,859 and 10,000.
This is the data-driven plan, not the plan of record. It models a volunteer as one person hosting one seven-minute sit. The team-based construct agreed on the 31 July morning call — captains building persistent teams, potential meditators included, schools at 300–500 — lives in mom-volunteer-mobilisation-plan.md and produces a different and larger minutes number. The death zone — and the three things that fix it

Yoga Veera went 1,000 signed → 350 trained → 60 who actually did it. The loss is not at signup and not at training. It is between being trained and doing it once.

  • First sit within 7 days of signup, cell leader physically present. Close the gap between joining and doing and most attrition never happens.
  • A human call within 48 hours. Not a WhatsApp broadcast. A person.
  • Visible attribution from day one. Every host sees their own minutes counted from their first sit — not a city total they cannot locate themselves in.
The weekly metric that matters is not signups.It is the share of new hosts completing a first sit within seven days. Below 50%, recruiting faster makes things worse — you are filling a bucket with a hole in it.

Four risks that can kill this

1 · In-app access slips past 1 NovemberOnly 170,776 of 293,569 Bengaluru MOM users have a phone number on record. Reaching the other ~123,000 requires in-app notification. Without it the realistic ceiling is 7,859 and 10,000 is unreachable by any amount of ground effort. This is not a dependency on the critical path — it is the critical path. Escalate in the first week of August. Parallel mitigation: start phone outreach to the contactable 170,776 in Phase 1.
2 · The 3% response rate is unvalidatedCarried from the grassroots plan; nothing in Ishangam confirms it. Test on Tier 1 in August, decide 15 September.
3 · Registration density is not engagement densityIshangam holds no sessions, minutes or last-active data, so the tiering ranks registrations — which may include people who opened the app once in 2025. Pincode 562114 shows 7,073 users against 117 active meditators; it is excluded, but only because it was large enough to see. Smaller artefacts are still in there.
4 · The definition driftsIf "volunteer" quietly reverts to meaning someone who gives weekends, every number here is wrong and nobody notices until November. Write the one-sit definition into the captain brief and the signup form.

Volunteer target is a stretch allocation, not a forecast. Each pool is given its historically-observed volunteering conversion rate — 8% for 6-month-active meditators (top of the observed 3–8% centre range), 3% for meditators not active in 6 months, 0.5% for potential meditators — giving an expected yield of 6,923 volunteers city-wide. The 16,000 target is then distributed across pincodes in proportion to that yield. Hitting 16,000 therefore requires 2.31× the conversion these pools have historically delivered. The per-pincode numbers sum to exactly 16,000 by construction; they are a fair split of an ambitious number, not evidence the number is reachable. Change the three rates and every target moves.

Three limits in the data — read before quoting any of this

Pincode is real, but only for the matched quarter of the base. ip_pincode is IP geolocation and useless — all 81,465 Bengaluru records carrying it share the same value, 562114, and nationally the top values are 110001 / 400017 / 411001, city GPO centroids. Real pincodes come from the contact_id_fkey → res.partner.zip join: 76,002 of the 128,393 centre-assigned Bangalore records match a contact, and 71,750 resolve to a Bangalore pincode. That is 24% of the city base. Reliable shape, unreliable magnitude — the estimate assumes matched users (people Isha already holds a contact record for) live where unmatched ones do.
There is no activity data. "Active app users" cannot be answered here. No sessions, no minutes, no last-active field. The only temporal field is first_login_ts, the registration date. The active boolean is Odoo's archive flag — all 2,392,695 records are true, so it means nothing. Usage still has to come from MOM core.
Two data-quality artefacts. Karnataka – Others holds 146,906 records, more than every named Bangalore centre combined — roughly half the Karnataka base is unassigned. Budigere Cross shows 17,610 users of whom 100% first logged in during 2026 and 8,320 since May, against only 170 meditators; at pincode level the same artefact lands as 7,073 matched users in 562114 (Hoskote) against 117 active meditators, a ratio 30× the city norm. Both are shown but excluded from every ranking and allocation.
