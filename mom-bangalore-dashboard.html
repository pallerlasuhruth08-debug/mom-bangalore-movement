MOM Bangalore — volunteer mobilisation

Live from Ishangam. Meditator, active-meditator and potential-meditator figures are an exact server-side query run 12 Aug 2026 (res.partner, 14 Bangalore centres). MOM app figures are the miracle.of.mind pull of 31 Jul 2026 — 2,392,695 records nationally, 293,569 in Bengaluru. Target: 16,700 signed hosts by 1 December 2026, yielding 10,000 active volunteers at 40% attrition.

Where do 16,700 signups come from?

10,000 active volunteers needs 16,700 signed hosts at 40% attrition. The centre system supplies 6,923 of them. The rest has to come from the app base — and only the part of it we can actually reach.
The one number that carries the argument
Insight 1 — the one that changes the plan

You are not recruiting from a growing community. You are reactivating a cold list.

73% of the entire Bangalore base — 213,648 of 293,569 people — registered in the first two months after launch, seventeen months ago. Since then the city has added about 5,000 a month, and 2026 is running slower than 2025 at 3,839.

  • The 3% assumption is far more fragile than it looks. Three percent of an engaged, growing community is a reasonable planning number. Three percent of a seventeen-month-old registration list that has never been contacted is a guess. The plan treats them as the same population.
  • Organic growth will not help. At the current run rate, August–November adds roughly 15,000 users — 5% on the base. Nothing in the December arithmetic can lean on new signups.
  • The campaign's real job is reactivation, not acquisition. "Come back" is a different ask from "join" — different copy, different first touch, lower expected response. Plan for it now rather than discovering it in October.
One bright spot worth chasing.October 2025 shows 9,488 signups against neighbouring months of 4,224 and 3,537. Something worked that month. It is the only evidence in eighteen months of a repeatable acquisition lever, and whoever ran it should be found this week.
Insight 2 — the cheapest pool, currently in nobody's plan

Miracle of Mind has not penetrated Isha's own base

Bangalore has 75,869 meditators in Ishangam — an exact count as of 12 Aug, not an estimate. Only 30,791 appear as MOM users. Matching is imperfect, so the honest reading is that roughly 40,000–45,000 Bangalore meditators are not on the app.

  • They already meditate — no persuasion needed about the practice
  • They are contactable through their centre — no dependency on the 1 November in-app date
  • Getting them on the app directly produces December minutes, whether or not they ever volunteer
  • It is a defensible ask for coordinators who may resist a general volunteering drive — get your own people onto the app is squarely their job

Of those meditators, 26,937 have transacted in the last six months — the warmest 36%, and the pool this plan leans on hardest at an assumed 8% signup rate worth 2,155 of the 16,700. Recommendation: make meditator app-onboarding a named workstream with a centre-wise target.

The city norm is about one to two app users per six-month-active meditator and roughly a third already being meditators. These two sit at 10–30× the ratio with almost no meditator overlap. Together they hold 61,249 users — 21% of the Bangalore base. They are excluded from the seeds; what is still missing is an answer to where these records came from.

Insight 4 — and the anomaly it reveals

Strip those two out and the centre model is unusually consistent

Every genuine Bangalore centre now sits between 0.2 and 4.0 app users per six-month-active meditator. That tightness is a gift — a per-centre model actually generalises, so you can set one centre's target from another's performance and expect it to hold.

It also makes the one genuine anomaly visible: Girinagar, with 959 six-month-active meditators but only 405 MOM users — far below the norm in the wrong direction. Either the centre never pushed the app or something structural is blocking it. The cheapest fix available: one conversation, not a campaign.

Insight 5 — good news for the pin-charge model

Referral behaviour is broad, not superstar-driven

4,322 Bangalore referrers produced 12,367 referrals. Top 10 account for 9.5%, top 100 for 22.5%, top 500 for 46.9%. The median referrer referred exactly one person; the largest referred 496.

A power law, but a mild one — no tiny elite carrying everything. The 1,088 people who have referred three or more are a genuinely broad recruiting base, not a handful of super-connectors who will be over-asked and burn out. A model depending on thousands of ordinary people each bringing a few others is exactly what this distribution supports.

It also argues for the plan of record's two-currency design. Rewarding teams spawned separately from minutes aims precisely at converting median referrers — people who have already brought one person — into people who bring five. That is where the volume is, not at the top of the list.

Insight 6 — reframes the 1 Nov date

Consent is not the constraint. Contact data is.

417 people out of 293,569 are on do-not-disturb — one in seven hundred — and 99.6% carry an explicit opt-in. But 42% have no phone number on record. The wall between you and the base is not permission or goodwill; it is missing contact data. In-app access is therefore not a channel preference, it is the only route to 123,000 people you are otherwise structurally unable to reach.

Cheap parallel play: every touchpoint between now and December — consecration, IE closings, school sessions, pincode meetups — should capture a phone number as a first-class action. Every number captured permanently converts an unreachable user into a reachable one.

Insight 7 — the target passes its first real check

16,700 survives contact with the data — but only with in-app access

Two independent routes now agree. Bottom-up from the exact 12 Aug pools — 26,937 six-month-active meditators at 8%, 48,932 other meditators at 3%, 660,008 potential meditators at 0.5% — the Isha base gives 6,923 signups. The MOM app base, minus those already counted as meditators, gives 7,883 with in-app notification. Together: 14,806.

The residual to 16,700 is 1,894 — close to the ~1,200 the grassroots plan had already assigned to consecration, IE closings and walk-ins, before any of this data existed. That is the first external check this target has had, and it broadly passes.

But the margin is thin and it is entirely a function of in-app access. Without it the app route yields only 4,586, the ceiling falls to 11,509, and the residual becomes 5,191 — several times what event capture can plausibly deliver. The sensible posture: hold 16,700 / 10,000 as the public numbers, plan the ground structure for 11,509 signups (6,905 active), and treat everything above that as contingent on in-app access landing on time.

Insight 8 — structure

Over-built at the bottom, under-built at the top

20 pincodes carry 49% of the signup target; 86 carry 21% between them. A flat hundred-captain grid spends identical effort on a pincode worth 0 signups and one worth 685. Cutting to 63 — dedicated captains with cell-leader depth in Tier 1, one each in Tier 2, Tier 3 clustered into 14 multi-pincode captaincies — redeploys that capacity where the signups are.

The top can be staffed from people already doing the job: 1,088 have referred three or more people unprompted, and every Tier 1 pincode has at least 9 — 519 candidates across Tier 1 alone. 26 pincodes have none and must be sourced from the centre.

  1. Escalate in-app access. It is worth 3,297 signups. With it the ceiling is 14,806; without it 11,509, against a 16,700 target.
  2. Publish 16,700 and retire 14,000 and 16,000. Both circulating numbers land short of 10,000 active at 40% attrition — 8,400 and 9,600 respectively.
  3. Open the meditator app-onboarding workstream. Roughly 40,000+ Bangalore meditators are not on the app. Cheapest minutes available, no dependency on 1 November.
  4. Test the 8% assumption on Tier 1 in August. The model needs 2.41× the blended historical rate. If the warmest pool converts at 4%, you need to know in September, not November.
  5. Get an answer on Bangalore Rural and Budigere Cross. 21% of the app base is currently uncountable.

The structure — span of 10, three or four layers

135 pin charges → 170 area POCs → 1,732 captains → 16,700 on-ground vols → 10,016 active layer 1: one per pincode layer 2: only in the 56 pincodes layer 3: ≤10 vols each layer 4: the signups at 40% attrition (Tier 3: ~6 pincodes per cluster lead) needing >10 captains (≤10 capts each)

Modelled on the BJP booth machinery, with a hard rule: no one holds more than 10 direct reports. A captain holds ≤10 on-ground vols; where a pincode needs more than 10 captains, an Area POC layer is inserted holding ≤10 captains each; the pin charge holds whichever layer sits below them, never more than 10 seats. 20 Tier 1 pincodes carry 49% of signups; Tier 3's 86 pincodes are grouped into ~14 clusters where one lead can hold several pin-charge seats. Total machinery above the vols: 2,037 people — that is itself a recruitment target, and it is why the captain layer leans on the 1,088 proven referrers. The death zone — and the three things that fix it

Yoga Veera went 1,000 signed → 350 trained → 60 who actually did it. That is 94% attrition, not 40%. The entire 16,700 → 10,000 arithmetic assumes you beat Yoga Veera by a factor of nine.

  • First sit within 7 days of signup, cell leader physically present. Close the gap between joining and doing and most attrition never happens.
  • A human call within 48 hours. Not a WhatsApp broadcast. A person.
  • Visible attribution from day one. Every host sees their own minutes counted from their first sit — not a city total they cannot locate themselves in.
The weekly metric that matters is not signups.It is the share of new hosts completing a first sit within seven days. Below 50%, recruiting faster makes things worse — you are filling a bucket with a hole in it. And if attrition lands at 50% rather than 40%, 16,700 signups yields 8,350 active, not 10,020.

Four risks that can kill this

1 · In-app access slips past 1 NovemberOnly 170,776 of 293,569 Bengaluru MOM users have a phone number. Reaching the rest requires in-app notification, worth 3,297 signups. Without it the ceiling is 11,509 against a 16,700 target — a gap of 5,191 that no ground effort closes. Escalate in the first week of August. Parallel mitigation: start phone outreach to the contactable 170,776 in Phase 1.
2 · The conversion rates are assumptions, not measurements8% / 3% / 0.5% are carried from the historical 3–8% centre volunteering range and a 3% app-base planning figure. Nothing in Ishangam confirms any of them for this campaign. The target needs 2.41× the blended rate they imply. Test on Tier 1 in August, decide 15 September.
3 · Six-month activity is a proxy for engagement, not engagementlast_txn_date records a transaction — a payment, a registration, a donation. 26,937 meditators transacted since 1 Feb 2026. Someone who meditates daily but has bought nothing counts as inactive; someone who made one donation counts as active. It is the best recency signal Ishangam holds, and it is still a proxy.
4 · The definition driftsIf "volunteer" quietly reverts to meaning someone who gives weekends, every number here is wrong and nobody notices until November. Write the one-sit definition into the captain brief and the signup form.

Three limits in the data — read before quoting any of this

Meditator, active-meditator and potential-meditator counts are exact. Everything about the MOM app is not. The three pool columns on the Pincode seed tab are a direct server-side read_group on res.partner run 12 Aug 2026 — 26,937 six-month-active meditators, 75,869 meditators, 660,008 potential meditators across the 14 real Bangalore centres, grouped on zip. No sampling, no estimation. Est. MOM users is the opposite: miracle.of.mind holds no pincode field at all, so that column is each pincode's share of a 71,750-record matched sample projected onto the 293,569 Bengaluru base — reliable shape, unreliable magnitude.
"Active in the last six months" means a transaction, not a meditation. last_txn_date records a payment, registration or donation. Someone who meditates every morning but has bought nothing since January counts as inactive; someone who made a single donation in March counts as active. It is the best recency signal Ishangam holds and it validates well — the 12-month version reproduces the previous active-meditator total to within 1.5% — but the 8% signup rate applied to these 26,937 people assumes transaction recency predicts willingness to volunteer. Nothing tests that.
There is still no app usage data. "Active app users" cannot be answered here. No sessions, no minutes, no last-active field on miracle.of.mind. The only temporal field is first_login_ts, the registration date. The active boolean is Odoo's archive flag — all 2,392,695 records are true, so it means nothing. Usage still has to come from MOM core.
Two data-quality artefacts. Karnataka – Others holds 146,906 records, more than every named Bangalore centre combined. Budigere Cross shows 17,610 app users of whom 100% first logged in during 2026, against only 1,116 six-month-active meditators; at pincode level the same artefact lands as 7,073 matched users in 562114 (Hoskote). Both are shown but excluded from every ranking and allocation.
